    Annual and irregular expenses

    My big irregular costs that the monthly budget does not carry: a long-term care premium, tax estimates, the trip. One twelfth of each is added to what I pay each month, but the twelfths stay in the money market: in the month a bill is due, the card adds the whole bill to that month's transfer to checking. "Starts" is the month of the next payment; "Ends" is the last one, blank if it goes on. An item only counts while it is running.     The draw rate is my yearly gap divided by everything I have invested, the same measure as the familiar 4% rule. The band is the rate on the day we set it, plus 20%. When the rate drops well under the baseline, that is a sign at the annual review that the distributions could rise.

    How my money works in retirement

    Each of us runs our own side. We each have a cash reserve, our own invested accounts, our own income, and a page like this one. The one thing we agree on together is how much each of us deposits into the shared checking account every month, which is our share of the household budget on the Planner.

    Three pots on each side. The shared checking account pays the bills. My money market, the reserve, holds two years of what I owe each month beyond my guaranteed income, and it is where the big once-a-year bills sit until they are due. My managed accounts, invested for growth, hold the rest.

    Every month, money comes to me from three places. Guaranteed income such as a pension, Social Security or wages, which arrives no matter what. A fixed distribution from my managed accounts, set once a year. And, if those two do not cover my deposit and my own spending, a top-up from my reserve.

    The reserve exists for bad years. If the market falls far enough, I stop the distributions so I am not selling investments while they are cheap, and live off the reserve instead. Two years of cover is long enough for most downturns to pass. When the market recovers, I restart the distributions and refill the reserve.

    One number tells me when. The draw rate: what I take from my investments in a year, as a share of what is in them. The familiar guidance is that about 4% is sustainable. I set a baseline when the plan starts. If the rate climbs more than a fifth above that, the pot has shrunk or spending has grown too much, and the card says to pause. When it comes back inside the band, the card says to restart.

    What the check-in is. Once a month, type in two balances, confirm what I pay and what comes in, and read the card. It says exactly what to do, including who to call. Then save the month. The log keeps the history.
