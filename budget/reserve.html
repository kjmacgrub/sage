    The draw rate is the yearly gap divided by the managed accounts. The band is the rate on the day we set it, plus 20%. When the rate drops well under the baseline, that is a sign at the annual review that the distributions could rise.

    How our money works in retirement

    Three pots. A checking account that pays the bills. A money market account, the reserve, that holds two years of what the bills cost beyond our guaranteed income. And the managed accounts, invested for growth, where the rest of our money lives.

    Every month, money arrives in checking from three places. The annuity and any wages or Social Security, which come no matter what. A fixed distribution from the managed accounts, set once a year. And, if those two do not cover the month, a small top-up from the reserve.

    The reserve exists for bad years. If the market falls far enough, we stop the distributions so we are not selling investments while they are cheap, and we live off the reserve instead. Two years of cover is long enough for most downturns to pass. When the market recovers, we restart the distributions and refill the reserve.

    One number tells us when. The draw rate: what we take from the managed accounts in a year, as a share of what is in them. We set it when the plan starts. If it climbs more than a fifth above that, the pot has shrunk or spending has grown too much, and the card on this page says to pause. When it comes back inside the band, the card says to restart.

    What the check-in is. Once a month, type in three balances, confirm what we spend and what comes in, and read the card. It says exactly what to do, including who to call. Then save the month. The log keeps the history so anyone can see how we have been doing.
