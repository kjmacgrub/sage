    The draw rate is my yearly draw divided by the Growth bucket, the same measure as the familiar 4% rule. The band is the rate on the day we set it, plus 20%. When the rate drops well under the baseline, that is a sign at the annual review that the distributions could rise.

    How my money works in retirement

    Three buckets. The Spending bucket is our shared checking account; it pays the bills. My Cash bucket is a money market account holding two years of what I put in, and it is where the big once-a-year bills wait until they are due. My Growth bucket is everything of mine that is invested, and it is left alone to grow.

    Each of us runs our own side. We each have a Cash bucket, a Growth bucket, our own income, and a page like this one. The one thing we agree on together is how much each of us deposits into the Spending bucket every month: our share of the household budget on the Planner.

    My draw. Whatever my deposit and my own spending come to, beyond my guaranteed income, is my draw: the part my buckets have to supply. The Growth bucket pays a fixed distribution into the Spending bucket each month, set once a year, and the Cash bucket covers the rest.

    The Cash bucket exists for bad years. If the market falls far enough, I stop the distributions so I am not selling investments while they are cheap, and live from the Cash bucket instead. Two years of cover is long enough for most downturns to pass. When the market recovers, I restart the distributions and refill the Cash bucket from the Growth bucket.

    One number tells me when. The draw rate: my draw for a year, as a share of the Growth bucket. The familiar guidance is that about 4% is sustainable. I set a baseline when the plan starts. If the rate climbs more than a fifth above that, the Growth bucket has shrunk or spending has grown too much, and the card says to pause. When it comes back inside the band, the card says to restart.

    What the check-in is. Once a month, open the numbers panel, type in the two bucket balances, confirm what I pay and what comes in, and save. Then read the card: it says exactly what to do, including who to call. The log keeps the history.

    If I am not here. The steps do not change. The only phone calls this page ever asks for are to the broker: pause the monthly distributions, restart them, or sell a stated amount from the Growth bucket into the Cash bucket. Anything bigger is an annual-review question, not a monthly one.
